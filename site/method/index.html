How it works

Four moves, one report.

A study runs on your category, not on a template. Nothing is simulated, and nothing is smoothed over.

  1. 01

    We write the questions

    Fifty questions your category is actually decided by, mined from docs, threads, reviews and RFPs. Written in the words people type, not the words on your homepage.

  2. 02

    We ask the agents

    Each question goes to Claude Code and Codex CLI three ways: plain, comparative and as a live use case. Same question, three different doors.

  3. 03

    We log every pick

    Which vendor came first, who was named after, who was never seen, and every page the agent read before it answered.

  4. 04

    You get the radar and the fixes

    Your category on one radar, the sessions behind every dot, and the pages that cost you the pick, ranked by leverage.

Studies are dated snapshots. We re-run the same question set, so the next radar shows movement instead of noise.

One question, three doors

The same question, asked three ways.

Phrasing changes the answer, and the answer changes who gets picked. So every question in a study is asked plainly, comparatively, and as a live use case.

Honest limits

Where the method has limits.

A study is a measurement, not a magic trick. These are the three places where it can be wrong, and what we do about them.

Agents change.

Claude Code and Codex CLI ship new versions. Every study is a dated snapshot, which is why we re-run the same question set instead of testing one-off prompts.

Small samples are marked, not merged.

In the sample study, Codex CLI returned 4 sessions out of 148. That line says too few to rate instead of becoming a number you would trust.

Competitor lines stay anonymized.

In this sample and in shared reports, other vendors appear as Brand X, Y and Z. Your study shows your own numbers, named.
